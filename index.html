What it does

Automatic export

iOS wakes Hozz when new Health data is recorded and sends it on. Set it up once and it keeps running for months without you opening the app.

Never loses a record

Hozz tracks exactly where it left off for every data type. Miss a day, reboot, force-quit — the next run picks up precisely where it stopped. No gaps, no duplicates, ever.

Destinations you own

A folder that syncs to your computer, or an HTTPS endpoint you run. iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Google Drive, a NAS — anything the Files app can reach.

Formats that fit

NDJSON for everything, losslessly. CSV for spreadsheets. JSON for scripts and AI tools. Plus a Health Auto Export-compatible payload, so an existing Home Assistant or Grafana setup keeps working when you point it here.

Honest about limits

Apple won't let any app read Health while your phone is locked, and iOS decides when background work runs. Hozz says so plainly instead of pretending sync is instant.

See it without installing anything

Drop an export into the web viewer and get charts for every data type. It runs entirely in your browser — the file never leaves your machine, because there is no server to send it to.

Private by construction

No account, no analytics, no telemetry, no relay. Credentials stay in your iPhone's Keychain. The code is open, so you don't have to take our word for it.

How it works

  1. Pick a destination

    Choose a folder, or paste in a web address you control. Tap Send a test and see the result immediately.

  2. Grant Health access

    You choose exactly which data types Hozz can read, in Apple's own permission sheet.

  3. Forget about it

    New data flows to your destination on its own. The dashboard always shows when it last succeeded.

Somewhere for it to land

A folder of files isn't really access to your health data. A database is. Hozz ships a receiver — one file, no dependencies — that turns batches into SQLite you can query with anything. 

# Watch a folder that already syncs here
python3 hozz_receiver.py watch ~/Dropbox/Health

# Or listen for a REST destination
python3 hozz_receiver.py serve --port 8765 --token secret

See the receiver

And a Mac app to catch it

Your phone sends straight to your computer over your own network. No server in the middle, no account, nothing to sign up for. Your Mac announces itself, so there's no IP address to type — and none to re-type when your router changes it.

It just finds it

Open Hozz on your Mac and it appears on your phone. Copy one token and you're done.

A real database

Years of history you can chart, filter and export to CSV — not a folder of files you have to write a script for.

Locked to your network

The receiver needs a token. Anything else on your Wi-Fi gets turned away, and nothing is ever sent outward.

Ask your own data a question

Hozz can hand your health data to an AI assistant that supports the Model Context Protocol — running on your Mac, reading from your Mac. Ask how your resting heart rate has moved this year, or what changed after you started sleeping earlier. 

You:  How did my resting heart rate trend this year?

Hozz: RestingHeartRate by month (count/min)
      2026-01  avg 58   min 54  max 63
      2026-02  avg 57   min 53  max 61
      ...

It can only read — nothing an assistant does can change or delete your records. And Hozz is straight with you about the trade: whatever the assistant reads goes wherever that assistant sends it. If it runs in the cloud, your health data goes to the cloud. That's worth knowing before you connect one.
